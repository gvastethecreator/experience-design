# Evidence and claims

## Two independent axes

**Basis type:** standard; empirical research; review/meta-analysis; professional guidance; heuristic; preference; product hypothesis. **Local evidence:** code; rendered state; execution; participant; analytics; simulation. A solid scientific source does not turn an uninspected problem into an observed one. A local execution does not prove a general human effect.

The source registry distinguishes `selected-text`, `abstract`, `metadata-only`, and blocked access. Do not say “I read the full study” if only its abstract was consulted. Do not elevate a commercial publication or an analogy to an experimental result. For sensitive decisions, find the current standard or the original work and read the passage that supports the claim.

## Chain of a recommendation

Reproducible observation → consequence for a task → possible explanation → concrete change → cost or risk → refutation test. The explanation can be uncertain even when the failure is real. Example: “the warning was not seen in this session” is an observation; “everyone suffers from banner blindness” is not.

Store the scope and the exact reference: route/state/viewport for visual; file and line for code; protocol and event for execution; pseudonymized session and moment for participant. Keep personal data out of the report by default. A bibliography link does not replace product evidence.

## Severity and confidence

P0: immediate serious harm or an unauthorized action with critical consequences. P1: blocking of a critical task, major loss, or material exclusion. P2: recoverable friction or an error of moderate cost. P3: a low-harm comprehension or craft adjustment. These are categories of this process, not a universal scientific scale.

High/medium/low confidence expresses support for the observation and for the mechanism. Describe known or unknown reach separately. Do not multiply severity by confidence to hide a severe, under-researched risk: prioritize verifying it. Expose disagreements and strengths that the proposal could destroy.

## Core corrections

- Miller/Cowan: memory under task conditions does not fix the number of visible controls (CO-WM).
- Hick: stimulus–response reaction does not prove that reducing every choice improves the product (CO-HICK; CO-HICK-REVIEW).
- Choice overload: consider moderators, not a ban on variety (CO-CHOICE).
- Zeigarnik: the 2025 meta-analysis found no general recall advantage for unfinished tasks; it distinguishes resumption from recall (CO-ZEIGARNIK).
- Aesthetics: assess appearance and performance separately (NN-AESTHETIC).
- Tesler, Occam, Pareto, and Parkinson: thinking tools, not universal obligations or reasons to remove critical cases.
- Fitts: a motor task model, not the sole justification for size, positioning, or conformance (CO-FITTS).
- Broad neuroscience: a theory of affect or pattern processing does not prove a UI pattern or a dopamine intervention. CO-PATTERN and CO-ATT are not trials of a specific interface.

## Decisions under conflict

Safety and authority → effective access → correctness and recovery → understanding and efficiency → expression and preference. This order guides; it does not replace context. Artistic or game work can seek challenge and surprise; distinguish intentional difficulty from opaque controls. Document the decision when two legitimate goals compete.

A negative result is useful. Do not adjust the criterion after seeing the favorite variant. Do not call “validated” what is only plausible, simulated, compiled, or visually attractive.
